Math, made friendlier

Let’s figure it out.

Pick a topic for a simple explanation, a worked example, and a couple of practice prompts.

＋
x²AlgebraMake friends with letters＋

The idea: A letter stands in for a number we don’t know yet. Keep both sides of an equation balanced.

Example
If x + 4 = 10, subtract 4 from both sides: x = 6.

Try it: What is y if y + 7 = 12?  ·  What is a if 3a = 18?

＝EquationsFind the missing value＋

The idea: Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Undo operations in reverse order.

Example
For 2x + 3 = 11, subtract 3, then divide by 2: x = 4.

Try it: Solve 2x = 14.  ·  Solve n − 5 = 9.

%PercentagesThink “out of 100”＋

The idea: “Percent” means “per hundred.” Turn a percentage into a decimal by dividing by 100.

Example
20% of 50 = 0.20 × 50 = 10.

Try it: Find 10% of 80.  ·  Find 25% of 40.

½FractionsParts of a whole＋

The idea: The denominator tells how many equal parts make a whole; the numerator tells how many parts you have.

Example
1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4. With matching denominators, add the numerators.

Try it: Simplify 4/8.  ·  Add 2/5 + 1/5.

△GeometryShapes, space & size＋

The idea: Perimeter measures around a shape; area measures the space inside it.

Example
A rectangle 5 cm by 3 cm has area 5 × 3 = 15 cm².

Try it: Find the perimeter of a 4 by 2 rectangle.  ·  Find its area.

↗GraphsRead the story in the data＋

The idea: Check the axes and their labels first. A coordinate is written (x, y): across, then up.

Example
The point (2, 3) is 2 steps across and 3 steps up from zero.

Try it: In (4, 1), which value is the y-coordinate?  ·  What does a rising line show?

English, one step at a time

Find the right words.

Clear explanations and examples for the bits of English that can feel tricky.

Aa
AaGrammarHow sentences fit together＋

The idea: Grammar is how words work together. A sentence usually needs a subject and a verb.

Example
The curious student (subject) asks (verb) a question.

✦VocabularyGrow your word garden＋

The idea: Learn a new word with its meaning, a sentence, and a word it connects to.

Example
Curious means eager to know or learn. “Mina was curious about the stars.”

◷TensesWhen did it happen?＋

The idea: Verb tenses tell when something happens: past, present, or future.

Example
I learn (now) · I learned (before) · I will learn (later).

✎WritingGet your ideas onto a page＋

The idea: Start with one clear point. Add details that support it, then finish by reminding the reader why it matters.

Example
Point → reason → example → a short wrap-up.

▤ReadingRead for the big picture＋

The idea: Preview the title, look for the main idea, and use nearby sentences to work out unfamiliar words.

Example
Ask yourself: “What is this paragraph mostly telling me?”

!Common mistakesLittle checks, big difference＋

The idea: Give your work one final read, checking that each sentence is complete and punctuation is in place.

Example
“Their going home” → “They’re going home.” They’re means they are.

Small habits, big help

Study tips that stick.

Little ways to make study time feel calmer and more useful.

☼
01

Make words memorable

Use a new vocabulary word in your own sentence. Come back to it tomorrow, then again next week.

02

Prep in small steps

Break a test topic into bite-sized parts. A short review across a few days beats one late-night cram.

03

Take notes your way

Write key ideas in your own words. Leave space to add questions, examples, or a tiny sketch.

04

Give focus a chance

Pick one small goal, put distractions aside, and try 20 focused minutes followed by a short break.

05

Beat the “later” feeling

Start with just five minutes. Opening your notes and doing the first tiny task is a real start.

The essentials, at a glance

Quick revision guides.

Short refreshers to help you remember the important bits.

▤
＋Fractions & percentagesOpen guide ↓

Fractions

For addition, make the denominators match. To simplify, divide top and bottom by the same number.

Part ÷ whole = fraction  ·  Fraction × 100 = percent

Example: 1/4 = 25%, because 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25.

Remember

“Of” often means multiply: 20% of 60 = 0.2 × 60 = 12.

＝Solving equationsOpen guide ↓

Keep it balanced

Use inverse operations to get the unknown by itself. Do the same thing to both sides.

2x + 4 = 10  →  2x = 6  →  x = 3

Remember

Check your answer by putting it back into the original equation.

AaSentence essentialsOpen guide ↓

Build a clear sentence

A complete sentence expresses a thought and usually has a subject and a verb.

Subject + verb + the rest of the idea.

Example

“The small dog chased its ball.” Subject: the dog. Verb: chased.

◷English tensesOpen guide ↓

Spot when it happens

Present: a habit or now. Past: already happened. Future: will happen later.

I read · I read yesterday · I will read tomorrow

Remember

Keep the tense consistent unless the time changes.
